\title{Remaining Useful Life Prediction on NASA C-MAPSS FD001: Engineered Degradation Features against Learned Sequence Models}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Sarvesh Srinath, Trishit Swarnakar, Aryan Kumar,
Husain Khorakiwala and Navodit Sharma}
\IEEEauthorblockA{Team T081, Section E, CSAI Project 7}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We predict the remaining useful life (RUL) of turbofan engines in the FD001
subset of NASA C-MAPSS and ask whether networks that learn from raw sensor
sequences can match or beat hand-engineered degradation features. Phase 1
computes rolling means, standard deviations, least squares slopes and a
learned health index within each engine and trains Elastic Net, Random
Forest and XGBoost on them. Phase 2 trains an LSTM, a temporal
convolutional network and a Transformer encoder on 30-cycle windows of the
14 informative sensors, and two hybrids that combine both kinds of input.
Every model is tuned by five-fold cross validation grouped by engine on RUL
capped at 125 cycles, the 100 test engines are scored once after every
choice is frozen, and each network is trained with five seeds. The selected
Transformer, which led XGBoost by 0.71 cycles in cross validation, reaches
a test RMSE of $15.37 \pm 0.66$ cycles against 15.36 for the selected
XGBoost; a paired bootstrap interval of $-2.3$ to $+2.2$ cycles shows that
the two are tied. Learning from raw sequences therefore matches, but does
not beat, the engineered features on FD001. The networks are more accurate
close to failure, with a late-stage RMSE of 5.88 cycles against 7.03 on
engines within 50 cycles of failure, and less accurate between 51 and 100
cycles. Adding the engineered features to the Transformer does not improve
it on the test set. Against test labels clipped at 125, our best models rank
seventh to tenth of 21 among our models and published FD001 deep learning
results.
\end{abstract}
